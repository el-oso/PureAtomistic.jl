The MACE model ​

PureMLIP.Model evaluates a ScaleShiftMACE on the CPU: energies, forces, virial and stress of a fully periodic structure.

Loading a model ​

julia
using PureMLIP
ck = load_mace_checkpoint("mace-mp-0b3-medium.model")
model = MACEModel(ck)            # Float64 parameters
model32 = MACEModel{Float32}(ck) # Float32 parameters

MACEModel{T}(ck) reads the channel, element, basis and radial-MLP sizes from the checkpoint and checks every other architectural choice against the supported architecture below. A difference in an architectural choice throws an ArgumentError that begins model: unsupported checkpoint and names the item found and the item expected; a tensor of the wrong shape throws an ArgumentError or DimensionMismatch with a model: prefix.

Public fields: z_table (the atomic numbers of the model's elements), r_max (cutoff, Å), e0 (reference energy of each element, eV), scale and shift.

The supported architecture is the one of MACE-MP-0b3 and MACE-MPA-0: a ScaleShiftMACE with one head, two density-normalized interactions (RealAgnosticDensityInteractionBlock, then RealAgnosticDensityResidualInteractionBlock), hidden irreps Cx0e + Cx1o, edge attributes 1x0e + 1x1o + 1x2e + 1x3o, correlation order 3 in both product blocks, a linear and a nonlinear readout, ZBL pair repulsion, the Agnesi distance transform on the Bessel radial basis, and normalize2mom(silu) activations.

Evaluating ​

julia
r = mace_energy_forces(model, positions, cell, atomic_numbers)
r.energy, r.forces, r.virial, r.stress

  • positions is 3 × n, one column per atom, in Å. Atoms need not lie inside the cell.

  • cell is 3 × 3 with the lattice vectors as its columns, in Å.

  • atomic_numbers holds one integer per atom; each must be an element of model.z_table.

  • The structure is periodic in all three directions. There is no non-periodic mode.

  • Energies are in eV, forces in eV/Å, the virial in eV and the stress in eV/Å³. node_energies holds the energy of each atom with its reference energy included.

The virial is -∂E/∂ε for the symmetric strain ε, and the stress is ∂E/∂ε / |det cell|: positive when the cell is under tension, so the stress is -virial / |det cell|. Both are symmetric 3 × 3 matrices. Their Voigt components (xx, yy, zz, yz, xz, xy) are the entries [1,1], [2,2], [3,3], [2,3], [1,3] and [1,2].

mace_energy_forces allocates its neighbor list, workspace and result. For repeated evaluations, allocate them once and call the in-place forms, which do not allocate:

julia
nl = neighbor_list(positions, cell, model.r_max; extra_capacity = 64)
ws = MACEWorkspace{Float64}(model, size(positions, 2), length(nl.senders); elements = atomic_numbers)
res = MACEResult{Float64}(size(positions, 2))
neighbor_list!(nl, positions, cell)
mace_energy_forces!(res, ws, model, positions, cell, atomic_numbers, nl)  # or mace_energy!

When only energies are needed, a MACEEnergyWorkspace takes the place of the MACEWorkspace in mace_energy!. It holds no derivatives and nothing for a backward pass, and runs the per-atom layers of both interactions on blocks of receivers, so it is smaller. Measured with Base.summarysize for MACE-MPA-0 in Float64 at the default edge_chunk = 4096 and block = 256, with 78 edges per atom: at 10,976 atoms of one element it takes 14,402 bytes per atom against 91,613 for a MACEWorkspace (89,557 without its contraction matrices). Each workspace also holds buffers whose size does not depend on the number of atoms or edges, about 63 MB for a MACEEnergyWorkspace and 132 MB for a MACEWorkspace at those settings, plus the MACEWorkspace's contraction matrices, 22.6 MB per element. They dominate small cells. A smaller edge_chunk and block shrink the fixed part. The energies are those of mace_energy_forces! up to rounding; the forces, virial and stress of the result are left as they were.

julia
ws_e = MACEEnergyWorkspace{Float64}(model, size(positions, 2), length(nl.senders))
mace_energy!(res, ws_e, model, positions, cell, atomic_numbers, nl)

elements is a required keyword: the atomic numbers the workspace can evaluate. The workspace holds the symmetric-contraction matrices only for the listed elements (for MACE-MPA-0, 22.6 MB per element in Float64 and 11.3 MB in Float32), built by the constructor; an evaluation on an atom of any other element throws an ArgumentError. With these matrices the first product block and the pullbacks of both run as matrix products over groups of 64 atoms of one element, through mul!, so an activated PureBLAS runs them.

MACEWorkspace{T}(model, n_atoms, n_edges; elements, nchunks) splits the atoms of an evaluation into nchunks receiver chunks, contiguous ranges of nearly equal size, each evaluated with its own buffers. Results for different nchunks agree up to rounding; for a given nchunks they are the same on every evaluation. With several chunks edge_chunk defaults to 1024 edges instead of 4096, since every chunk holds its own edge-chunk buffers. Each chunk after the first then adds, for MACE-MPA-0 in Float64 at the default block, about 55 MB (25.5 MB of edge-chunk buffers, 15 MB of block buffers and 15 MB of symmetric-contraction scratch) plus 4,096 bytes per atom: 112,029 bytes per atom with 4 chunks and 148,560 with 8 at 10,976 atoms of one element, against 91,613 with one; the chunks share the contraction matrices.

Passing ThreadedChunks() as the last argument of mace_energy! or mace_energy_forces! runs the receiver chunks of the forward and the backward pass in parallel on the threads Julia was started with: chunks 1 to nchunks - 1 on worker tasks that the workspace keeps from one evaluation to the next, the last on the calling task. The sums over the edges that give the forces and the virial run on the calling task. The results are the same bits as without ThreadedChunks() for the same nchunks, on any number of threads. The speed-ups in PureMLIP/bench/results/ (thread_scaling_*, ab_threaded_*) were measured under julia -t n,0 --gcthreads=1,0 (no interactive thread, one garbage-collection thread) with nchunks = n at 2, 4, 6 and 8 threads on an Apple M6 (2 + 4 fast cores and 6 efficiency cores); past 6 threads the extra tasks run on efficiency cores. Each task runs its own matrix products, so a multithreaded BLAS should be limited to one thread (BLAS.set_num_threads(1) with OpenBLAS; PureBLAS runs one thread until PureBLAS.set_num_threads is called).

The first evaluation with ThreadedChunks() on a workspace starts the workers, and with PureBLAS each worker allocates PureBLAS's task-local matrix-product scratch once. The bytes that first evaluation allocates, measured with @allocated inside a function on cu_bench(2) (32 atoms) with four threads on Apple arm64 (macOS), Julia 1.12.7 and 1.13.3, are, for both precisions and both mace_energy!andmace_energy_forces!:

They are the tasks, channels, locks and vectors of the workers, and the same in three fresh processes on each Julia version. The tests gate these figures exactly on macOS arm64 and as an upper bound of twice the figure on every other platform, where they have not been measured. With PureBLAS the first evaluation allocates megabytes, because each worker's scratch of matrix-product buffers is retained: 11.1 MB (1 worker), 21.1 MB (3 workers) and 27.5 MB (7 workers) in Float64, and 5.6, 10.8 and 13.8 MB in Float32, the same in every process. The total allocated is larger than the retained scratch and varies between processes for mace_energy_forces! (Float64: 23.4 to 37.6 MB with 4 chunks and 31.4 to 45.1 MB with 8, over eighteen processes; Float32, 4 chunks: 11.9 to 17.9 MB), so the tests gate a range there: per precision and chunk count, from 512 KiB to 1.4 times the largest total seen, on macOS arm64 only; on other platforms, where PureBLAS's blocking differs and the totals have not been measured, they gate an upper bound of twice that total. The range is temporary: it stands in for an exact figure while PureBLAS keeps its scratch per task. What the extra, unretained bytes are has not been traced. With mace_energy! the totals were the same in all three processes: 1.65, 4.21 and 8.45 MB in Float64 and 0.83, 2.14 and 4.20 MB in Float32 for 2, 4 and 8 chunks. The allocation profiler lists the small objects of the workers (tasks, channels, locks) but not the scratch vectors, whose data buffers it does not record. The tests show that later evaluations allocate nothing, with OpenBLAS and with PureBLAS: the fewest bytes of five evaluations is 0 (a single evaluation can allocate up to 192 bytes more when a thread first runs a function in a task, an entry in the runtime's method cache). Idle workers wait without running. They stop when the workspace is no longer reachable, or at once with close(ws); a later threaded evaluation starts them again. The neighbor list of a threaded evaluation must have the element type of the model. A workspace serves one evaluation at a time: a threaded phase started on ws while another runs, or close(ws) during one, throws.

Evaluation on a GPU ​

With KernelAbstractions loaded (and Metal, CUDA or AMDGPU for a GPU), the PureMLIPGPUExt extension evaluates a model on the device that holds its arrays:

julia
using KernelAbstractions, Metal  # KernelAbstractions loads the extension
backend = MetalBackend()
P, C = Float32.(positions), Float32.(cell)
dm = PureMLIP.device_model(model32, backend; elements = unique(atomic_numbers))
dP, dC = MtlArray(P), MtlArray(C)
dnl = PureMLIP.device_neighbor_list(dP, dC, model32.r_max; extra_capacity = 1000)  # built on the device
n = size(P, 2)
ws = PureMLIP.device_workspace(dm, n, dnl.capacity)
res = PureMLIP.device_result(dm, n)
mace_energy_forces!(res, ws, dm, dP, dC, MtlArray(Int32.(atomic_numbers)), dnl)
neighbor_list!(dnl, dP, dC)  # after the positions have changed, on the device

device_neighbor_list(positions, cell, r_cut; skin, extra_capacity) builds the list on the device with the grid of the CPU's neighbor_list, so its edges, their order and the map from each edge to its reverse equal those of neighbor_list(positions, cell, r_cut + skin) in the same precision; PureMLIP.device_neighbor_list(nl, backend) copies a host list instead. neighbor_list! rebuilds it on the device when an atom has moved by more than half the skin since the last build or the cell has changed (every call when the skin is 0, the default), and reads back only the list's status word. When the edges exceed the capacity it throws, and the list holds no edges until it is built anew with device_neighbor_list; an evaluation with such a list throws the same error. The default skin is 0 because a rebuild costs under 1 % of an evaluation on Metal (bench/results/gpu_neighbor_skin_metal.json).

A caller that rebuilds the list and evaluates in one step and reads the status word back once for both (the MD step of PureAtomistic) uses the parts of neighbor_list! and mace_energy_forces!: PureMLIP.device_rebuild!(dnl, dP, dC) and PureMLIP.device_energy_forces!(res, ws, dm, dP, dC, dZ, dnl) launch their kernels and read nothing back, PureMLIP.read_device_status!(ws, dnl) reads the status word, and PureMLIP.check_device_status(ws, dm, dZ, dnl) throws for every flag except an overflow of the capacity, whose edge count it returns so that the caller can build a larger list (device_neighbor_list(...; extra_capacity_fraction) sizes it as a fraction of the edges found) and evaluate again. PureMLIP.device_sum!(out, x, n) is the fixed-order device sum of the energy.

The results stay on the device; Array(res.forces) copies them to the host. The forces are gathered per atom over the edges it receives, through the list's map from each edge to its reverse, and the energy and virial are fixed-shape tree sums, so an evaluation uses no atomic operations and repeats bit for bit. Metal has no Float64, so a model on Metal is a MACEModel{Float32}. The device backward pass supports at most 256 channels; device_model throws an ArgumentError for a model with more, before any launch.

The device mace_energy!, mace_energy_forces! and neighbor_list! are the second exception to "! functions do not allocate": each call allocates host memory for its kernel launches, its matrix products and the one read of the device's status word. Measured on cu_bench(2) (32 Cu atoms, MACE-MPA-0, one element, the workspace defaults; for neighbor_list! a 6 Å cutoff, a call that rebuilds and one that keeps the list), as the fewest bytes of five calls:

backendmace_energy!mace_energy_forces!neighbor_list! (rebuild / keep)

The tests gate these figures as upper bounds per backend. The device memory does not grow: on Metal the allocated device memory reads the same after 10 and after 210 evaluations.

The neighbor list ​

neighbor_list(positions, cell, r_cut) returns the directed edges (sender, receiver, shift) with 0 < ‖positions[:, receiver] - positions[:, sender] + cell * shift‖ < r_cut, sorted by receiver. The model requires r_cut >= model.r_max and edges sorted by receiver, as neighbor_list returns them, and throws an ArgumentError otherwise. Its capacity is the number of edges found plus extra_capacity; neighbor_list! rebuilds the list for new positions in place and throws when the new edge count exceeds the capacity, in which case the list must be rebuilt with neighbor_list. A workspace is sized for a number of atoms and edges and is used with any structure within them; for repeated evaluation it must cover the list's capacity, length(nl.senders), not only the current edge count nl.n_edges.

Float32 ​

MACEModel{Float32} computes in Float32 end to end. The arguments of the evaluation functions must have the model's element type. Against LAMMPS-MACE on the reference structures, the largest Float32 energy difference is 1.6e-5 eV on an energy of -235 eV, and the largest force difference is 9.4e-6 eV/Å.

Using PureBLAS ​

The matrix products of the model go through LinearAlgebra.mul!, so PureBLAS can carry them without any change to PureMLIP:

julia
using PureBLAS
PureBLAS.activate()

Every PureBLAS speed measurement so far comes from one Apple M6 machine on one thread; there is no measurement on another machine yet. The PureBLAS example shows the measured speed-ups, read from the committed benchmark records.

PureBLAS requires Julia 1.13 and is not registered. The tests use commit c38f273f; add that commit with Pkg.add(url = "https://github.com/el-oso/PureBLAS.jl", rev = "c38f273fd75807811e647e5dc69ae85dbaceeaf6"). PureBLAS.activate() reroutes the BLAS calls of the whole process, not only PureMLIP's, and PureBLAS.deactivate() restores the previous libraries. PureMLIP does not depend on PureBLAS. The tests in PureMLIP/test/pureblas run with PureBLAS active. They check the reference energies, forces and virials, computed with mace_energy_forces, and that mace_energy! and mace_energy_forces! with a MACEWorkspace do not allocate, also with ThreadedChunks() on four threads once the workers have started, where they give the bits of the serial evaluation; mace_energy! with a MACEEnergyWorkspace is not covered there.

PureMLIP.Model.NeighborList Type
julia
NeighborList{T}

Directed edges (sender, receiver, shift) between atoms of a fully periodic cell, with r_cut the cutoff in Å. Edge e with e ≤ n_edges carries the vector positions[:, receivers[e]] - positions[:, senders[e]] + cell * shifts[:, e], of length 0 < ‖r‖ < r_cut, and the edges are sorted by receiver. senders, receivers and shifts have the capacity as length; only the first n_edges entries are valid. The remaining fields are the scratch of the cell grid.

neighbor_list(positions, cell, r_cut; extra_capacity = 0) -> NeighborList

Edges within r_cut of a fully periodic system. positions is 3 × n (one column per atom, Å), cell is 3 × 3 with the lattice vectors as columns. Positions need not lie inside the cell. The capacity is the number of edges found plus extra_capacity.

Rebuild nl for new positions and cell without allocating. After a capacity error nl must be rebuilt with neighbor_list: its scratch already describes the new positions. Throws before changing the edges if more than length(nl.senders) edges are found.

A ScaleShiftMACE model of the MACE-MP-0b3 / MACE-MPA-0 architecture with parameters of type T, read from a checkpoint; any other architecture throws model: unsupported checkpoint. PureMLIP.Model.MACEWorkspace Type
julia
MACEWorkspace{T}(model, n_atoms, n_edges; elements, nchunks = 1, edge_chunk, block = 256)

The buffers of an evaluation of model on up to n_atoms atoms and n_edges directed edges. Per edge it holds the edge vector, its length and the energy gradient with respect to the edge vector; the spherical harmonics, the radial features and the radial-MLP outputs (the tensor-product weights) are recomputed from them edge_chunk edges at a time. Each receiver chunk (below) holds its own buffers of edge_chunk edges, so edge_chunk defaults to 4096 with one chunk and to 1024 with several. For MACE-MPA-0 on 500 atoms, one chunk and one thread, mace_energy_forces! takes 1.4 to 2.6 % less time with 1024 edges at a time than with 4096 (OpenBLAS and PureBLAS, Float64 and Float32; bench/results/ab_edge_chunk_1024_*).

The atoms of an evaluation are split into nchunks receiver chunks: contiguous ranges of nearly equal size, chunk c of n atoms being div((c - 1)n, nchunks) + 1 : div(cn, nchunks). Each chunk has its own buffers, so no buffer is written by two chunks: the edge-chunk buffers above, the block buffers below, the symmetric-contraction scratch, and the derivative with respect to the sender features of the second interaction, which the edges of a chunk add to at arbitrary senders. Within a chunk, the summed messages of each interaction run on block receivers at a time, in buffers of min(block, cld(n_atoms, nchunks)) atoms; so does the per-atom linear layer of the first interaction, while that of the second interaction writes a per-atom buffer that the backward pass reads. Each further chunk adds its edge-chunk and block buffers and one per-atom array of the sender-derivative size; with nchunks = 1 there is one set. The results for different nchunks agree up to rounding, and are the same for a given nchunks. With ThreadedChunks() the chunks run as tasks.

The first product block (forward and pullback) and the pullback of the second run as matrix products over groups of up to 64 atoms of one species, with dense matrices per element (PureMLIP.Tensors.ContractionMatrices): for MACE-MPA-0 22.6 MB per element in Float64 and 11.3 MB in Float32. elements is required: the atomic numbers (repeats allowed) of the elements the workspace can evaluate. The workspace holds matrices only for these elements, built by the constructor, and an evaluation on an atom of any other element throws. Each receiver chunk holds the buffers of one group (14.2 MB in Float64 for MACE-MPA-0).

The workspace also holds the energy derivatives of the backward pass of mace_energy_forces!. The evaluation functions require T to be the element type of the model. Apart from the contraction matrices of elements, which need T to be the element type of the model, the constructor reads only sizes from model: with elements = (), T may also be a number type the layers accept, such as a dual number.

source
PureMLIP.Model.MACEEnergyWorkspace Type
julia
MACEEnergyWorkspace{T}(model, n_atoms, n_edges; edge_chunk = 4096, block = 256)

The buffers of mace_energy! on up to n_atoms atoms and n_edges directed edges, without forces: it holds no derivative and nothing kept for a backward pass, so it is smaller than a MACEWorkspace of the same sizes. For MACE-MPA-0 in Float64 at the default edge_chunk and block it is about six times smaller at 10,976 atoms and about two times at 256 atoms, where buffers whose size does not depend on the atom count dominate. Per edge it holds the edge vector and its length; the spherical harmonics, the radial features and the radial-MLP outputs are recomputed from them edge_chunk edges at a time. Per atom it holds the sender features of both interactions, the skip connection of the second interaction and the linear readout; every other per-atom layer runs on block receivers at a time, in buffers of min(block, n_atoms) atoms. mace_energy! with this workspace fills res.energy and res.node_energies and leaves the forces, virial and stress of res as they were.

PureMLIP.Model.ThreadedChunks Type
julia
ThreadedChunks()

Passed as the last argument of mace_energy! or mace_energy_forces! with a MACEWorkspace, it runs the receiver chunks of the workspace in parallel on the threads Julia was started with: chunks 1 to nchunks - 1 on tasks that the workspace keeps (its workers), the last chunk on the calling task. The chunks write disjoint parts of the workspace and of the result, and every sum over chunks is taken in chunk order on one task, so the results are the same bits as without ThreadedChunks() for the same workspace, whatever the number of threads. An evaluation runs in phases, each started after every chunk has finished the one before: interaction 0 and product 0; interaction 1 and the readouts, after which the energy is summed; and for mace_energy_forces! the pullback of interaction 1, in which each chunk adds to its own derivative with respect to the sender features, and the pullback of interaction 0, in which each chunk first sums those derivatives at its own atoms in chunk order. The forces and the virial are then summed over the edges on the calling task. The neighbor list must have the element type of the model. A workspace serves one evaluation at a time: a threaded phase that starts while another runs on the same workspace, or close(ws) during one, throws an ArgumentError instead of mixing up the answers of the workers.

The first evaluation with ThreadedChunks() starts the workers and allocates them; later evaluations hand them their phases and allocate nothing. Idle workers wait without running and hold no thread. They stop when the workspace is no longer reachable, or at once with close(ws); a later evaluation starts them again. A worker whose chunk throws ends, the evaluation rethrows its error (as a TaskFailedException) once every chunk has finished, and the next evaluation replaces the worker.

Each task runs its matrix products through the BLAS library on its own thread, so a multithreaded BLAS should be limited to one thread (BLAS.set_num_threads(1) with OpenBLAS). A BLAS that keeps scratch per task (PureBLAS) allocates it in each worker once; the workers keep it from one evaluation to the next.

Base.close Method
julia
close(ws::MACEWorkspace)

Stop the tasks that run the receiver chunks of ws in evaluations with ThreadedChunks(), and wait until they have ended. ws stays usable; its next threaded evaluation starts them again. Without close they stop when ws is no longer reachable. A workspace serves one evaluation at a time, so close(ws) while a parallel phase of a threaded evaluation runs on ws throws an ArgumentError.

PureMLIP.Model.mace_energy! Function
julia
mace_energy!(res, ws, model, positions, cell, atomic_numbers, nl[, ThreadedChunks()]) -> res

Evaluate model on the periodic structure with positions (3 × n, Å), lattice vectors as the columns of cell, and atomic_numbers, over the edges of the neighbor list nl (built for these positions with a cutoff of at least model.r_max, and with its edges sorted by receiver as neighbor_list sorts them). Fills res.energy and res.node_energies: E0 of the element plus scale · (pair + readouts) + shift per atom. ws is a MACEWorkspace or, when no forces will follow, the smaller MACEEnergyWorkspace, which leaves the forces, virial and stress of res as they were. A MACEWorkspace must have been built with every element of atomic_numbers in its elements; with it, ThreadedChunks() runs its receiver chunks as tasks.

source
PureMLIP.Model.mace_energy_forces! Function
julia
mace_energy_forces!(res, ws, model, positions, cell, atomic_numbers, nl[, ThreadedChunks()]) -> res

As mace_energy!, and also fill res.forces (3 × n, eV/Å, -∂E/∂positions), res.virial (-∂E/∂ε for the symmetric strain ε, eV) and res.stress (∂E/∂ε / |det cell|, eV/Å³, positive when tensile). The derivatives come from a backward pass through the layers: with g_e = ∂E/∂r_e for edge vector r_e, the sender of e gets +g_e and the receiver -g_e in the forces, and ∂E/∂ε = sym(Σ_e r_e g_eᵀ). With ThreadedChunks(), the forward and the backward pass run the receiver chunks as tasks, and these sums over the edges run on the calling task.

mace_energy_forces(model, positions, cell, atomic_numbers) -> MACEResult
