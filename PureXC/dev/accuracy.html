Accuracy and speed ​

Every figure on this page is a bound or a measurement stated in a committed test or record of the repository; each table names its source.

Oracle grid ​

Source: PureXC/test/libxc/grid.jl.

  • ρ, the total density: 131 points log-spaced over [1e-10, 1e3];

  • s, the reduced gradient: 0 and 41 points log-spaced over [1e-3, 10];

  • ζ, the spin polarization: −1, −(1 − eps), −0.6, 0, 0.25, 0.95, 1 − eps, 1 (eps = eps(Float64)).

A polarized GGA point has the same s in both channels and parallel gradients, σαβ = √σαα·√σββ. A point is above when it is above every threshold of the functional (every term, for a sum), and below otherwise. Every gated comparison takes the above points; the rule items of the main suite gate the below points.

Float64 against Libxc and BigFloat ​

Source: PureXC/test/libxc/runtests.jl, run by the CI job purexc-libxc. The oracles are Libxc 7.0.0 at orders 0 to 2 and the same Julia functional converted exactly to 256-bit BigFloat. Every component of every output field is compared in Libxc's layout. Where the BigFloat value of a component is exactly 0, its magnitude is replaced by the largest magnitude in its field at that point.

Float32 ​

Source: PureXC/test/libxc/runtests.jl. A Float32 call is compared with the exact function of the Float32 functional (its parameters rounded to Float32), so the comparison measures the evaluation error alone. The bound, at orders 0 to 4, is

max(|jl32 − big32| − floatmin(Float32), 0) ≤ c_32·eps(Float32)·max(1, κ̂)·|big32|,

with κ̂ the Float64 error at the same point in ulps of Float64: where the formula is ill-conditioned, the Float64 evaluation shows it, and the Float32 bound grows with it. An infinity is accepted only where the exact value is beyond floatmax(Float32) with the same sign; no other output may be non-finite. The finiteness check takes every grid point and, below the grid, the densities from 1e-12 to 1e-11 with σ from 0 to 1e-28 (band_points), where the terms of pbe at order 2 each exceed floatmax(Float32) with opposite signs. Each order is read from a call of that order, so it is measured in that call's evaluation type: pbe is evaluated in Float32 at orders 0 and 1 and in Float64 above them, with each output rounded once (its ratios at orders 2 to 4 are at most 0.50). The constant and the largest ratio measured (C_32):

Known differences ​

Source: PureXC/test/libxc/runtests.jl (KNOWN_COUNT). At a polarized point within one rounding of 1 ± ζ = ε_ζ, one evaluation keeps the factor (1 ± ζ)^p and another replaces it by the constant of the threshold rule, so their derivatives differ by the series part of that factor. These points are excluded from the gated items and listed with their count and largest differences. They are exactly the grid rows ζ = ±(1 − eps):

Bounds of the main suite ​

The main suite compares every functional with its exact BigFloat function at orders 0 to 10, at sweeps of densities, spin splits and reduced gradients up to the domain limits. The bounds are in eps of the result type: for the LDA relative to the largest entry of each output, for the PBE functionals per entry against the scale of its order. Where a row lists a measurement, it is the largest error that PureXC/test/scans/point_bounds.jl measures over every point it covers. The PBE bounds above order 3 are about 10 times that measurement; the LDA bound at orders 4 to 10 is about 17 times its largest measurement.

The 8κ terms are the conditioning of 1 ± ζ formed from ζ: where one channel is far smaller than the other, κ is large, and errors of order 1 relative are the documented conditioning of the formula, not an evaluation error.

Speed against Libxc ​

Source: PureXC/bench/results/libxc_speed_mac.json, written by PureXC/bench/libxc_speed.jl. Apple M6, Julia 1.13.4, Libxc 7.0.0 through Libxc.jl 0.3.27, one thread in each arm, 10^6 points drawn from the above points of the oracle grid, PureXC at commit 532a2e4. Each of 7 rounds runs one child process per arm, in alternating order, and reports the median of three samples per cell; a fixed calibration loop discards rounds in which the children ran at different speeds (none was discarded). A cell computes every order from 0 to k into preallocated outputs, and the PureXC arm includes its input check.

Speed-up is the Libxc time over the PureXC time, so above 1 means PureXC is faster: the median and [min, max] over the rounds. The Float32 column divides Libxc's Float64 time on the same points (Libxc runs in Float64 only) by PureXC's Float32 time. The gate is on the Float64 cells: the minimum speed-up of each must be above 1.

Known bias: Libxc evaluates pbe as gga_x_pbe and gga_c_pbe into separate outputs and does not sum them, while PureXC returns the sum, so the Libxc arm of the pbe cells does less work than the PureXC arm. How much this changes the pbe speed-ups is not measured. The times are those of one machine; other machines have not been measured.
