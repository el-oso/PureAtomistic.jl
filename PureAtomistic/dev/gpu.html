Molecular dynamics on a GPU ​

With KernelAbstractions loaded (and Metal, CUDA or AMDGPU for a GPU), the PureAtomisticGPUExt extension runs the whole velocity Verlet step on the device that holds the system's arrays.

Usage ​

julia
using PureAtomistic, PureMLIP, Metal, Random
model = MACEModel{Float32}(load_mace_checkpoint(path))
host = PureAtomistic.Systems.fcc_cell(3.615f0, 5, :Cu)
sys = PureAtomistic.to_device(host, MetalBackend())
p = PureAtomistic.device_potential(model, sys; skin = 0)
sim = Simulation(sys, p, VelocityVerlet(2.0); rng = Xoshiro(0))
run!(sim, 1000; every = 100, observer = s -> (copy_state!(host, s.system); println(total_energy(s))))

The kicks and the drift are kernels with the arithmetic of the CPU step, so for the same forces they give the CPU's bits. A step copies no array between host and device: the host reads back the status word of the neighbor list once (p.readbacks counts every read), and kinetic_energy, total_energy and temperature of the simulation each read one scalar summed on the device. copy_state! copies the positions, velocities and cell to a host system at the output interval.

When a step packs more edges than the device list has room for, the potential builds a new list and workspace with extra_capacity_fraction spare edges (default 0.05), evaluates again, counts it in p.n_regrows and reports it on standard error. That step reads the status word twice (both counted in p.readbacks), and the new list reads its edge count and its status word back while it is built (not counted). The old list and workspace stay allocated until the garbage collector frees them. Spare edges cost evaluation time: on Metal at 10,976 atoms, 10 % costs 5.6 % per evaluation, so the default 5 % costs about 2.8 %. In 2,000 steps of 2 fs of 500 Cu atoms near 1300 K at constant volume the edge count stayed within 3.5 % below and 0.005 % above that of the first step, and a rebuild took 8 ms against 68 ms for an evaluation (bench/results/gpu_capacity_margin_metal.json), so a rare regrowth is cheap.

BussiThermostat, LangevinBAOAB and BerendsenBarostat run on the device too. Their random numbers come from the counter-based generator Philox4x32-10 (J. K. Salmon et al., SC'11), drawn in the kernels: each step takes one key from sim.rng, and each normal number has its own counter (atom and component for the Langevin noise), so a seeded run repeats bit for bit on one device and a regrowth does not move the generator. Normal numbers come from the Box–Muller transform and the gamma variate of the Bussi step from the Marsaglia–Tsang method, as on the CPU. Device runs match CPU runs in their statistics, not in their random numbers. The Bussi step sums the kinetic energy and computes its scale factor on the device, and the BAOAB step runs its O step as a kernel, so a thermostatted step reads back only the status word. The barostat computes the pressure on the device and reads it back as one scalar, so a barostatted step reads twice; rescale! scales the cell and the positions on the device. Under the barostat the edge count follows the volume: in 2,000 steps of 2 fs of 500 Cu atoms near 1300 K at 0, 10 and 30 GPa, the volume ranged from 0.906 to 1.106 times its first value and the edge count from 12.5 % below to 3.8 % above, and the default 5 % spare edges never regrew the list (bench/results/gpu_barostat_regrow_metal.json).

A step allocates host memory for its kernel launches and the status read, an exception to the rule that ! functions do not allocate: 2,464 bytes on the KernelAbstractions CPU backend and about 457,000 bytes on Metal for the tiny test checkpoint, both gated by the GPU test suite, which also checks that the device memory in use stays the same over 1,000 steps. With MACE-MPA-0 on 32 Cu atoms a step allocates 2,400 bytes on the CPU backend and about 2.02 MB on Metal, almost all of it the evaluation's matrix products.

What stays on the device ​

The positions, velocities, cell, masses and atomic numbers, the neighbor list, the model's workspace, the energy, forces and stress, the kinetic-energy sums, the thermostat and barostat arithmetic and the random numbers all live on the device. Per step the host does three things: it launches the kernels, draws one 64-bit key from sim.rng when a thermostat is present, and reads back the status word of the neighbor list (plus one scalar for the barostat's pressure). The observer copies the state to a host system with copy_state! only at the output interval.

Validation on Metal ​

PureAtomistic/bench/md_validation.jl runs the validations of the CPU driver through this path with PUREATOMISTIC_DEVICE=Metal (Float32, MACE-MPA-0, skin 0), and stores the records as md_*_f32_metal.json in PureAtomistic/bench/results/. Each record carries the verdicts of criteria and compare_to_baseline against the Float32 records of the CPU. The thermostat noise of the device comes from Philox and that of the CPU from Xoshiro, so the device runs are statistically independent of the baseline.

The records hold every number: bench/results/md_nve_conservation_f32_metal.json, md_thermostat_check_f32_metal.json and md_vacancy_hop_f32_metal.json. The vacancy run has the simulated length of the CPU Float32 baseline, 440 ps, and the neighbor list never regrew in any run. The 32 fs run is the edge of stability, so its failing absolute criterion is the expected result and what is compared is that it fails where the baseline fails.

The absence of regrowth under the barostat (0, 10 and 30 GPa, see above) is specific to the 6 Å cutoff of MACE-MPA-0 lying between the fifth and sixth fcc shells. At a lattice constant a those shells lie at a √(5/2) and a √3, so the cutoff stays between them for 3.464 Å < a < 3.795 Å, that is volumes from 0.88 to 1.16 times that of a = 3.615 Å. A larger change of the volume, or another cutoff, carries a shell across the cutoff and changes the edge count abruptly.

Failures ​

A thermostat that fails on the device (a gamma sampler at its cap, or a Bussi step at zero kinetic energy) sets a flag in the status word of the neighbor list, and the next status read throws. The flag is sticky: every later status read of that potential throws, and the scalar reads of the energy queries and of the barostat do not look at it. Unlike the CPU, which throws before it changes anything, the Bussi step at zero kinetic energy has made its first half kick and its drift when the exception is raised, and sim.step_count has not advanced.
