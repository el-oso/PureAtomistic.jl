Trajectory analysis ​

PureAtomistic.Analysis computes structural and transport quantities from a run: the radial distribution function g(r) and its running integral, coordination numbers, the mean-square displacement and the diffusion coefficient, and the root-mean-square deviation from a reference.

Each function reads frames, any collection with eachindex and getindex whose elements have positions (3 × n, Å), cell (3 × 3, lattice vectors in columns) and species: a vector of Systems, a vector of IO.Frames, or a TrajectoryReader, which reads one frame per index. Cells may be triclinic. Positions, cells and species are read through their own axes, so views and OffsetArrays are accepted and give the same results as plain arrays. The arrays are read element by element on the host.

julia
using PureAtomistic, PureAtomistic.Analysis, PureAtomistic.IO
traj = TrajectoryReader("run.patraj")
(; r, g, n) = rdf(traj, 6.0, 120)                     # g(r) to 6 Å in 0.05 Å bins
z = coordination_numbers(traj[1], 3.0)                # neighbors within 3 Å, per atom
(; lag_fs, msd_A2) = msd(traj; max_lag = 500, nblocks = 5)
(; D, σ_D) = diffusion_coefficient(lag_fs, msd_A2; fit_window = (10_000, 50_000))  # Å²/fs

Periodic images ​

A pair search to a distance r_max uses the minimum image when r_max is at most half the shortest cell height: every pair closer than that is found once, at its true distance, in any cell shape. A longer r_max visits every periodic image within it, including an atom's own images, as in the infinite periodic crystal. rdf normalizes the pair count of each shell by the count of an ideal gas of the same composition. Within the minimum-image range an atom never pairs with itself, so the ideal count per unit volume is (N_A N_B - N_AB) / V, with N_AB the atoms selected by both members of the pair, and an ideal gas gives g = 1 on average for every selection, nothing included. Beyond it the count is N_A N_B / V, because the periodic images of the B atoms, an atom's own images included, fill space at the density N_B / V; g then tends to 1 at large r in any cell, and a species with a single atom works.

The mean-square displacement unwraps the positions frame to frame: the displacement between consecutive frames is taken under the minimum image. That is right only if every atom moves less than half the shortest cell height between frames, and choosing a frame interval short enough is the caller's responsibility. unwrap! throws for a displacement in the ambiguous range, but a longer move maps onto a shorter image and passes unnoticed: in a cubic cell of side L, a move of 0.7 L along an axis reads as -0.3 L. The frames are split into blocks, and msd returns one column per block; diffusion_coefficient fits the Einstein relation ⟨|Δr|²⟩ = 6 D τ + c to each column over the fit window and returns the mean and its standard error over the blocks. With few blocks that error is itself uncertain, by a fraction of about 1/√(2(nblocks - 1)).

rmsd takes positions as given, with no periodic images, and with align = true removes the best translation and proper rotation (Kabsch's method) before measuring the deviation.

Example: atomic diffusion by a vacancy at 1200 K ​

The first 1200 K production run of the vacancy-diffusion campaign: one vacancy in 4 × 4 × 4 conventional fcc cells (255 Cu atoms) with MACE-MPA-0 in Float32, 700 ps of constant-volume dynamics with the Bussi thermostat, a trajectory frame every 100 fs. Its VacancyTracker counted 140 hops. The MSD of all atoms has one block per 100-ps segment (7 blocks), lags up to 50 ps, and the fit runs over lags of 10 to 50 ps.

The hop count predicts the atomic diffusion coefficient. Each vacancy jump moves one atom by a nearest-neighbor vector of length λ = a/√2, so after n = Γt jumps the squared displacements of the N atoms sum to nλ² plus the cross terms of successive jumps of the same atom. Those are negative, because an atom that has just exchanged with the vacancy is likely to exchange back; their long-time effect is the tracer correlation factor f. With Σᵢ |Δrᵢ|² = 6 N D t,

D = f Γ λ² / (6 N) = f Γ a² / (12 N).

For the infinite fcc lattice f = 0.78145 (Compaan and Haven, 1956; the value is not taken from the source). The record measures f for this periodic cell by a lattice random walk of one vacancy among 255 atoms in the same 4 × 4 × 4 lattice, with the center-of-mass displacement removed as in the MD run, whose total momentum is zero; it reproduces the infinite-lattice value within its error.

A known limit: Γ counts the hops that the vacancy tracker confirms, and a jump that is undone within the tracker's dwell time counts as no hop. Such a pair moves no atom and leaves D unchanged, but the lattice walk's f includes it, so the prediction is low by the fraction of hops missed this way, which is not measured.

This page does not read the trajectory. bench/trajectory_diffusion.jl computes the record bench/results/trajectory_diffusion_T1200_dt2_s01.json from the run's trajectory and its campaign record, and the code below reads it.

julia
using CairoMakie, JSON, Printf, PureAtomistic
d = JSON.parsefile(joinpath(pkgdir(PureAtomistic), "bench", "results", "trajectory_diffusion_T1200_dt2_s01.json"))
@printf("%s: %d atoms, %.0f K, %d frames %g fs apart, %d hops\n", d["run"], d["natoms"], d["temperature_K"], d["frames"], d["frame_interval_fs"], d["hops"])
@printf("hop rate Γ = %.4f ± %.4f /ps, a = %.4f Å, λ = %.4f Å\n", d["rate_per_ps"], d["rate_se_per_ps"], d["lattice_constant_A"], d["jump_length_A"])
@printf("f in this cell = %.4f ± %.4f (infinite lattice %.5f)\n", d["correlation_factor_cell"], d["correlation_factor_cell_se"], d["correlation_factor_infinite"])
@printf("D atoms, from the MSD:      %.3e ± %.3e Å²/ps\n", d["D_atoms_A2_per_ps"], d["D_atoms_se_A2_per_ps"])
@printf("D atoms, from the hop rate: %.3e ± %.3e Å²/ps\n", d["D_atoms_predicted_A2_per_ps"], d["D_atoms_predicted_se_A2_per_ps"])
@printf("difference / combined error: %.2f\n", d["z_measured_minus_predicted"])
@printf("largest center-of-mass shift over the run: %.3f Å\n", d["com_max_shift_A"])

The two estimates agree within their errors. The MSD error comes from the spread of seven block estimates, so it is itself uncertain by about 30 % (the standard deviation of 7 values); the prediction's error is the Poisson error of 140 hops. Units: 1 Å²/ps is 10⁻⁴ cm²/s.

julia
lag = Float64.(d["msd_lag_ps"])
m = Float64.(d["msd_mean_A2"])
w = (d["fit_window_fs"][1] / 1000) .<= lag .<= (d["fit_window_fs"][2] / 1000)
slope = 6 * d["D_atoms_A2_per_ps"]
c = sum(m[w] .- slope .* lag[w]) / count(w)
pred = 6 * d["D_atoms_predicted_A2_per_ps"]
cp = sum(m[w] .- pred .* lag[w]) / count(w)
fig = Figure(; size = (760, 420), fontsize = 13)
ax = Axis(fig[1, 1]; xlabel = "lag (ps)", ylabel = "mean-square displacement (Å²)", title = "255 Cu atoms with one vacancy, 1200 K")
lines!(ax, lag, m; label = "MSD, mean of 7 blocks")
lines!(ax, lag[w], c .+ slope .* lag[w]; linestyle = :dash, label = "6Dτ + c, D from the MSD fit")
lines!(ax, lag[w], cp .+ pred .* lag[w]; linestyle = :dot, label = "6Dτ + c, D from the hop rate")
axislegend(ax; position = :rb)
fig

The rise at the shortest lags is the vibrational amplitude of the atoms, which the intercept c absorbs; the slope beyond it is the diffusion carried by the vacancy.

Reference ​

PureAtomistic.Analysis.rdf Function
julia
rdf(frames, r_max, nbins; species_pair = nothing) -> (r, g, n)

Radial distribution function of the pairs (A, B) = species_pair (nothing: all atoms, either element may be nothing) over frames, in nbins bins of width Δ = r_max / nbins up to r_max (Å). r holds the bin centers. With C_k the ordered-pair count of bin k summed over the frames (see rdf_histogram!, which also states when the minimum image is used),

julia
g_k = C_k / (Σ_frames P / V · 4π/3 (r_hi³ - r_lo³)),

the count divided by the count an ideal gas of the same composition would give, with P the number of ordered pairs the search can form in a frame. With the minimum image (r_max at most half the shortest cell height) an atom never pairs with itself, so P = N_A N_B - N_AB, where N_AB is the number of atoms selected by both A and B (N_A for one species twice, 0 for two distinct species): an ideal gas gives g = 1 on average for every selection. Beyond that range every periodic image is counted, an atom's own images included, as in the infinite periodic system, so P = N_A N_B: the images of the B atoms fill space at the density N_B / V, g tends to 1 at large r in any cell, and a species of one atom works.

n is the running coordination number, the integral ∫₀^{r_hi} ρ g 4πr² dr in histogram form: n_k = Σ_{j ≤ k} C_j / Σ_frames N_A, the mean number of B neighbors of an A atom closer than the upper edge of bin k.

frames is any collection with eachindex and getindex whose elements have positions, cell and species (a vector of Systems or Frames, or a TrajectoryReader). Views and OffsetArrays are accepted; r, g and n are plain vectors over the bins.

PureAtomistic.Analysis.rdf_histogram! Function
julia
rdf_histogram!(hist, positions, cell, species, r_max; species_pair = nothing) -> hist

Adds to hist the ordered pairs of one frame: for every atom a of the first species of species_pair and every periodic image of every atom b of the second species (nothing selects all atoms), except b = a at zero translation, a pair at distance r < r_max adds one to bin 1 + floor(r / Δ) with Δ = r_max / length(hist), the bins counted from firstindex(hist). The search uses the minimum image when r_max is at most half the shortest cell height and visits every image within r_max otherwise. positions is 3 × n, cell 3 × 3 with the lattice vectors in columns, and species is indexed like the atoms. Allocates nothing.

PureAtomistic.Analysis.coordination_numbers Function
julia
coordination_numbers(frame, r_cut; species_pair = nothing) -> Vector{Int}

Coordination numbers of one frame (anything with positions, cell and species, such as a System or a Frame): see coordination_numbers!. With species_pair = nothing the result has one entry per atom, indexed like the atoms (an OffsetArray frame gives an OffsetArray); with species_pair = (A, B) it has one entry per atom of species A, in atom order, as a 1-based vector. Throws if no atom has species A. The running integral of g(r), the mean coordination number as a function of distance, is the n returned by rdf.

PureAtomistic.Analysis.coordination_numbers! Function
julia
coordination_numbers!(counts, positions, cell, species, r_cut; species_pair = nothing) -> counts

Writes into counts (indexed like the atoms) the number of neighbors of each atom closer than r_cut (Å): for an atom of the first species of species_pair, the atoms of the second species and their periodic images, itself excluded at zero translation; nothing selects all atoms. Atoms that are not of the first species get 0. Images are found as in rdf_histogram!: the minimum image when r_cut is at most half the shortest cell height, every image within r_cut otherwise. Allocates nothing.

PureAtomistic.Analysis.msd Function
julia
msd(frames; species = nothing, max_lag, nblocks = 5, dt = nothing) -> (lag_fs, msd_A2)

Mean-square displacement ⟨|u(t₀ + τ) - u(t₀)|²⟩ of the atoms of species (nothing: all atoms) over the unwrapped positions u (see unwrap!), for lags τ = 0, 1, …, max_lag frames. The frames are split into nblocks consecutive blocks of B = nframes ÷ nblocks frames (the last nframes - nblocks·B frames are not used), and column b of msd_A2 averages over the atoms and every time origin t₀ of block b whose pair (t₀, t₀ + τ) lies inside the block, so the columns use disjoint stretches of the trajectory. lag_fs holds τ · Δt.

frames is any collection with eachindex and getindex whose elements have positions, cell and species (a vector of Systems or Frames, or a TrajectoryReader), with the same atoms and species in every frame (checked). The frame times come from each frame's time (fs), which must be evenly spaced, or, when dt is given, are dt apart. The frames must be close enough that no atom moves half the shortest cell height between two of them (see unwrap!); the caller is responsible for that. Positions in Å give msd_A2 in Å².

source
PureAtomistic.Analysis.unwrap! Function
julia
unwrap!(unwrapped, previous, positions, cell) -> unwrapped

Advances the unwrapped positions by one frame: for each atom adds to unwrapped the minimum-image displacement from previous to positions in cell (lattice vectors in columns), then copies positions into previous. The three matrices are 3 × n with equal axes. Every atom must have moved less than half the shortest cell height since previous, which the caller ensures by the frame interval: a longer move maps onto a shorter image without notice. Throws if a minimum-image displacement is itself at least half the shortest cell height long, since its image is then ambiguous. Allocates nothing.

PureAtomistic.Analysis.diffusion_coefficient Function
julia
diffusion_coefficient(lag, msd; fit_window) -> (D, σ_D)

Diffusion coefficient from the Einstein relation in three dimensions, ⟨|Δr|²⟩ = 6 D τ at long lags. lag holds the lags and msd one column per block, as returned by msd. For each block, the least-squares line with intercept through the points with fit_window[1] ≤ lag ≤ fit_window[2] gives D_b as its slope over 6; the intercept absorbs the vibrational plateau. D is the mean of the D_b and σ_D its standard error, std(D_b) / √nblocks, which treats the blocks as independent; σ_D is itself uncertain by a fraction of about 1/√(2(nblocks - 1)) (29 % for 7 blocks). Needs at least two blocks and two lags in the window. Units are those of msd per unit of lag (Å²/fs from msd; 1 Å²/fs is 0.1 cm²/s).

source
PureAtomistic.Analysis.rmsd Function
julia
rmsd(frames, reference; align = false) -> Vector

Root-mean-square deviation √(Σᵢ |xᵢ - yᵢ|² / n) of each frame's positions (xᵢ) from the 3 × n matrix reference (yᵢ), as a vector in the order of eachindex(frames). With align = true, each frame is first superposed on the reference by the translation and proper rotation that minimize the deviation (Kabsch's method): both are centered on their centroids and the frame is rotated by the optimal rotation. Positions are taken as given, with no periodic images, so a crystal should be passed with unwrapped positions. The positions of every frame must have the axes of reference; views and OffsetArrays are accepted.
