Vacancy diffusion in Cu ​

The migration energy E_m and prefactor Γ₀ of one vacancy in Cu with MACE-MPA-0, from the hop rates at 900, 980, 1080 and 1200 K, and the diffusion coefficients at each temperature. The method was fixed before any run (docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-08-vacancy-campaign-proposal.md).

Each run holds one vacancy in 4 × 4 × 4 conventional fcc cells (255 atoms), in Float32 on the GPU (Metal) with a 2 fs time step. It starts with 20 ps of Bussi thermostat and Berendsen barostat at zero pressure; the cell is then fixed at the mean volume of the last 10 ps, 2 ps of constant-volume dynamics are discarded, and the production run follows at constant volume with the Bussi thermostat (τ 100 fs). A VacancyTracker reads a frame every 20 fs (window 5, dwell 3) and counts the confirmed hops; a trajectory frame is stored every 100 fs. There are two independent runs per temperature, run as two processes at once, and one 499-atom run (5 × 5 × 5 cells) at 1200 K checks the cell size.

This page runs no dynamics. It reads bench/results/vacancy_campaign_analysis.json, which bench/vacancy_analysis.jl computes from the run records in bench/results/vacancy_campaign/, the NEB record bench/results/neb_vacancy.json and the per-run MSD records bench/results/trajectory_diffusion_<run>.json (computed by bench/trajectory_diffusion.jl from the stored trajectories, which are not in the repository).

julia

Hop rates ​

Per temperature the two runs are pooled: Γ = n/t with the Poisson error √n/t. T is the mean kinetic temperature of the production runs and a the lattice constant set by the barostat stage. "Transients" counts the episodes in which the tracker saw no single vacancy for at most 500 fs (a jump in progress) and skipped those frames.

The "longest gap" is the longest stretch without a confirmed hop, counting from the start and to the end of production; its p-value is the exact probability that n hop times spread uniformly over the run leave a gap at least that long.

One run stands out. T1080_dt2_s06 has no hop in its first 143 ps, against a mean waiting time of 12 ps at 1080 K; its segment counts give p = 0.002 and the gap p = 1.7 × 10⁻⁴. Over the eight runs, the chance that some run shows a gap that unlikely is about eight times larger, 1.3 × 10⁻³. In the stored trajectory frames (every 100 fs), assigning each atom to its nearest site, without the tracker's window, leaves the same one site empty from the start to 143.3 ps, the time of the tracker's first hop, with no excursion in any frame. The dwell is real; its cause is not known. The run is kept as it is; with the 1080 K rate taken from T1080_dt2_s05 alone the fit below changes by 0.001 eV.

Arrhenius fit ​

arrhenius_fit fits ln Γ = ln Γ₀ − E_m/(k_B T) with weights n (the inverse variances of ln Γ for Poisson counts) and does not rescale the errors by χ².

Comparison with the NEB barrier ​

The climbing-image NEB (bench/results/neb_vacancy.json, 255 atoms, Float64, fixed cell) gives 0.704 eV at the 0 K lattice constant 3.6404 Å and 0.621 eV at 3.7263 Å, the zero-pressure lattice constant at 1200 K: the barrier falls as the lattice expands. The fit runs at zero pressure, so its lattice constant grows with T, from 3.7006 Å at 900 K to 3.7277 Å at 1200 K. The NEB barrier is taken as linear in a through its two points (the linearity between them is assumed, not measured):

for t in Ts
    k = string(Int(t["nominal_K"]))

The barrier at the lattice constant of each temperature is about 0.63 eV, 0.11 eV (3.2 σ) below the fit. That comparison leaves out the expansion. With x = 1/(k_B T), a rate Γ = ν exp(−x E_m(a(x))) has the Arrhenius slope

E_eff = −d ln Γ / dx = E_m(a) + x (dE_m/da)(da/dx) = E_m(a) − T (dE_m/da)(da/dT).

With dE_m/da < 0 and da/dT > 0, the second term is positive: the barrier is lower at the temperatures where the rate is higher, so the rate rises faster with T than the barrier at any one lattice constant implies. For a linear in T, E_eff equals the barrier at the lattice constant extrapolated along a(T) to T = 0. The same term moves the intercept: the fitted Γ₀ is ν exp(T (dE_m/da)(da/dT)/(k_B T)). The script fits the rates that the NEB line and the measured a(T) predict with a constant ν, with the weights of the data, and finds:

@printf("fit − E_eff = %.3f eV = %.2f σ\n", fit["E_m_eV"] - neb["E_eff_expected_eV"], neb["z_fit_minus_E_eff"])
@printf("ln Γ₀ shift = %.2f, so ν = %.0f /ps, %.2f THz per jump direction (12 neighbors)\n", neb["ln_prefactor_shift"], neb["attempt_rate_implied_per_ps"], neb["attempt_frequency_per_jump_THz"])

The fit agrees with the expansion-corrected NEB barrier within 0.5 σ. This comparison sets the barrier's own temperature dependence at fixed volume (vibrational entropy, anharmonicity) and the Float32 to Float64 difference to zero; at the error of the fit, 0.034 eV, the data do not resolve them. The 7.6 THz per jump direction is of the size of a phonon frequency of Cu; it is derived from the fit, not measured on its own.

The 1300 K runs at the 0 K lattice constant (bench/results/md_vacancy_hop_f32{,_metal}.json, about 9 GPa) are not part of the fit. Diffusion coefficients ​

Two estimates of each diffusion coefficient. For the atoms, the hop rate predicts D = f Γ λ²/(6N) = f Γ a²/(12N) with the jump length λ = a/√2 and the tracer correlation factor f of the periodic cell, measured by a lattice walk of one vacancy in the same cell (see Trajectory analysis); the MSD of all atoms over the stored trajectory gives D directly (one block per 100 ps segment, lags up to 50 ps, fit over 10 to 50 ps). For the vacancy, D_v = Γ a²/12 against the MSD of the tracked vacancy position. The MSD values are the mean of the two runs.

These are diffusion coefficients at one vacancy in 255 sites; the self-diffusion coefficient of Cu also needs the equilibrium vacancy concentration, which the campaign does not measure.

Published values ​

Two experimental values were checked against the abstracts of their papers:

  • Simmons and Balluffi, Phys. Rev. 129, 1533 (1963): equilibrium vacancy concentrations from length and lattice-parameter expansion; the results imply a monovacancy migration energy of 0.88 ± 0.13 eV, with an assumed formation entropy.

  • Berger, Ockers and Siegel, J. Phys. F 9, 1023 (1979): the formation enthalpy 1.30 ± 0.05 eV from resistometry of quenched samples, which with the self-diffusion activation enthalpy gives a migration enthalpy of 0.77 eV; the activation enthalpy of post-quench annealing in the same work is 0.76 ± 0.04 eV.

The compilation value 0.70 ± 0.02 eV often quoted from Ehrhart (Landolt-Börnstein III/25) has not been checked against the source. The fit's 0.742 ± 0.034 eV lies within the range of the checked values and within one error of 0.76 ± 0.04 eV. The experimental values are enthalpies at zero pressure from lower temperatures (quenching, annealing) or combine several measurements; the fit is the zero-pressure Arrhenius slope over 900 to 1200 K of one interatomic potential.

Limits ​

  • Float32 on the GPU throughout; the NEB barriers are Float64. The Float32 and Float64 hop counts have been compared on 5 hops only.

  • The barrier's own temperature dependence at fixed volume is not separated from the expansion term; at the fit's error it is consistent with zero.

  • The tracker counts confirmed hops. A jump undone within its dwell time counts as none; the agreement of the diffusion coefficients bounds such misses only at the 10 to 20 % level.

  • The 4 fs run at 1200 K (T1200_dt4_s02) is kept as data and not used: 263 hops in 1296 ps, rate ratio to 2 fs 1.015 (95 % interval 0.82 to 1.25), stopped by a 520 fs saddle transient of a concerted two-atom jump.
